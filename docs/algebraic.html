Algebraic quantum theory

This page collects resources on operator algebras and their use in quantum theory: the mathematics of C*-algebras and von Neumann algebras, the algebraic formulation of quantum mechanics, quantum statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, and the use of von Neumann algebras in quantum gravity since about 2021. It continues the mathematical side of the guide to the foundations of quantum mechanics.

Each entry is tagged. Survey means article length, read in a sitting or two. Textbook means written to be learned from in order. Reference means a treatise to be consulted. Free means legally available online.

Where to start

Jonathan Sorce, “Notes on the type classification of von Neumann algebras,” Reviews in Mathematical Physics 36 (2024): 2430002 (doi) — survey, free (arXiv:2302.01958). Why factors are the basic case; the types I, II and III explained by whether the algebra has pure states, a trace, or neither, and by what it takes to renormalize an unnormalizable trace; standard forms; and the connection with modular theory, including the argument that local algebras in quantum field theory are type III1. Written for physicists reading the recent quantum-gravity papers listed below; it aims at intuition rather than precise definitions.

Edward Witten, “Notes on some entanglement properties of quantum field theory,” Reviews of Modern Physics 90 (2018): 045003 (doi) — survey, free (arXiv:1803.04993). The Reeh–Schlieder theorem, the modular operator, relative entropy and its monotonicity, all motivated by the finite-dimensional case. Its stated aim is to explain entanglement when it is a property of the algebra of observables and not just of the state.

Christopher Fewster and Kasia Rejzner, “Algebraic quantum field theory — an introduction,” in Finster, Giulini, Kleiner and Tolksdorf (eds.), Progress and Visions in Quantum Theory in View of Gravity (Birkhäuser, 2020) — survey, free (arXiv:1904.04051). The GNS construction, unitarily inequivalent representations (worked through the van Hove model), the axioms, the spectrum condition, Reeh–Schlieder, the split property, the universal type of local algebras and superselection sectors, with exercises.

Detlev Buchholz and Klaus Fredenhagen, “Algebraic quantum field theory: objectives, methods, and results” — survey, free (arXiv:2305.12923). Written for the Encyclopedia of Mathematical Physics. An overview of the whole program by two of the people who built it.

Hans Halvorson and Michael Müger, “Algebraic quantum field theory,” in Butterfield and Earman (eds.), Philosophy of Physics (North-Holland, 2007): 731–864 (doi) — survey, free (arXiv:math-ph/0602036). Nonlocality, the particle and field concepts, and inequivalent representations, followed by a detailed account of Doplicher–Haag–Roberts superselection theory; Müger’s appendix gives the categorical background for the Doplicher–Roberts reconstruction of the field algebra and gauge group.

Vaughan Jones, Von Neumann Algebras (lecture notes, Berkeley, 2009) — textbook, free (pdf). The fastest way into the mathematics. Built from semester courses; the exercises carry part of the exposition, and the notes get to examples and live topics with as little technical apparatus as possible.

The mathematics

First books

V. S. Sunder, An Invitation to von Neumann Algebras (Springer, Universitext, 1987) — textbook. A short book that gets to the modular theory quickly: the Murray–von Neumann classification, then Tomita–Takesaki theory and KMS states, crossed products, and Connes’s classification of type III factors.

William Arveson, An Invitation to C*-Algebras (Springer, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 39, 1976) — textbook. The basic ideas of C*-algebras and their representations, presented as simply and concretely as possible: Hilbert spaces are separable and algebras GCR wherever that helps, and a general theorem is often proved only in a special case that shows the idea, with the generalization left to the exercises.

Kehe Zhu, An Introduction to Operator Algebras (CRC Press, 1993) — textbook. A concise introduction to C*-algebras and von Neumann algebras, used as a course text.

Gerard J. Murphy, C*-Algebras and Operator Theory (Academic Press, 1990) — textbook. A first- or second-year graduate course, assuming basic functional analysis but no operator theory: spectral theory, C*-algebras and their representations, von Neumann algebras, tensor products, and an introduction to K-theory.

Kenneth R. Davidson, C*-Algebras by Example (AMS and Fields Institute, Fields Institute Monographs 6, 1996) — textbook. The theory taught through its examples: AF algebras, the Toeplitz algebra, Cuntz and Bunce–Deddens algebras, irrational rotation algebras, group C*-algebras and discrete crossed products. Many exercises.

Li Bing-Ren, Introduction to Operator Algebras (World Scientific, 1992) — textbook. Self-contained, and further-reaching than most introductions: Tomita–Takesaki theory, the classification of type III factors, reduction and decomposition theory, AF algebras, crossed products and Jones index theory.

Kenneth R. Davidson, Functional Analysis and Operator Algebras (Springer, CMS/CAIMS Books in Mathematics, 2025) — textbook. About 800 pages. Part I is a one-semester course in functional analysis; part II, Banach algebras and C*-algebras, then C*- and von Neumann algebras at a more advanced level; part III, topics in operator theory.

Textbooks and treatises

Richard Kadison and John Ringrose, Fundamentals of the Theory of Operator Algebras (4 vols., Academic Press, 1983–1992; vols. I–II reprinted by the AMS, 1997) — textbook. Volumes I and II are the course; volumes III and IV work every exercise.

Şerban Strătilă and László Zsidó, Lectures on von Neumann Algebras (2nd ed., Cambridge–IISc, 2019) — textbook. The first edition appeared in 1979 and went out of print quickly. The second adds the factors of ICC groups, the Murray–von Neumann proof that a type II1 factor has a trace, the first examples of type II1 and type III factors, and the theorems on algebras with a cyclic and separating vector. Its sequel is Şerban Strătilă, Modular Theory in Operator Algebras (2nd ed., Cambridge, 2020) — reference. First published in 1981, and until Takesaki’s second volume the only comprehensive monograph on the subject.

Masamichi Takesaki, Theory of Operator Algebras I–III (Springer, Encyclopaedia of Mathematical Sciences 124, 125, 127; 2002–2003) — reference. Volume I reissues the 1979 book. Volume II is modular theory, weights and crossed products; volume III, injective factors and their classification.

Bruce Blackadar, Operator Algebras: Theory of C*-Algebras and von Neumann Algebras (Springer, Encyclopaedia of Mathematical Sciences 122, 2006) — reference. One volume covering operators on Hilbert space, C*-algebras, von Neumann algebras, further structure, and K-theory, with proofs often sketched rather than given. The quickest place to look something up.

Gert K. Pedersen, C*-Algebras and Their Automorphism Groups (2nd ed., ed. Søren Eilers and Dorte Olesen, Academic Press, 2018) — reference. The 1979 classic, which develops C*-algebras as far as possible without Hilbert space representations, extended with commentary and a bibliography of the forty years since. The last two chapters treat automorphism groups and their spectral theory: the mathematics of dynamical systems on C*-algebras.

Jacques Dixmier, C*-Algebras (North-Holland, 1977) and Von Neumann Algebras (North-Holland, 1981) — reference. English translations of the French originals of 1964 and 1957 (2nd ed. 1969).

Karen Strung, An Introduction to C*-Algebras and the Classification Program (Birkhäuser, 2021) — textbook. Starts from Banach algebras and ends at the Elliott invariant, the Jiang–Su algebra, the Cuntz semigroup and the classification theorem for simple nuclear C*-algebras.

Nathanial Brown and Narutaka Ozawa, C*-Algebras and Finite-Dimensional Approximations (AMS, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 88, 2008) — textbook. Nuclearity, exactness, tensor products of C*-algebras and approximation properties: the background for the Connes embedding problem below.

Claire Anantharaman and Sorin Popa, An Introduction to II1 Factors (book draft) — textbook, free (pdf). Type II1 factors from the first examples onward, including Popa’s deformation/rigidity techniques.

Modular theory and crossed products

Fumio Hiai, Lectures on Selected Topics in von Neumann Algebras (EMS Press, 2021) (doi) — textbook, free as an earlier version (arXiv:2004.02383). A fast track through the theory as Araki, Connes, Haagerup and Takesaki left it in the 1970s: Tomita–Takesaki theory, the standard form, Connes cocycle derivatives, operator-valued weights, Takesaki duality, Haagerup’s Lp-spaces and spatial derivatives. The machinery behind relative entropy in quantum field theory.

Stephen J. Summers, “Tomita–Takesaki modular theory” — survey, free (arXiv:math-ph/0511034). Written for the first edition of the Encyclopedia of Mathematical Physics (Elsevier, 2006). The theorem, the KMS condition, and the applications to physics, in a few pages.

Hans-Jürgen Borchers, “On revolutionizing quantum field theory with Tomita’s modular theory,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 41 (2000): 3604–3673 (doi) — survey. Half-sided modular inclusions, the Bisognano–Wichmann theorem and the geometric action of modular groups, by one of its main contributors.

Dana P. Williams, Crossed Products of C*-Algebras (AMS, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 134, 2007) — textbook. The standard text on C*-crossed products by locally compact groups: covariant representations, imprimitivity theorems, induced representations and ideal structure, Takai duality, and the reduced crossed product. Assumes some familiarity with C*-algebras and Morita equivalence.

Alfons van Daele, Continuous Crossed Products and Type III von Neumann Algebras (Cambridge, London Mathematical Society Lecture Note Series 31, 1978) — textbook. Sixty-eight pages. Part I is crossed products of von Neumann algebras, the commutation theorem and duality; part II shows that the crossed product of a type III algebra by its modular automorphism group is semifinite, and derives the structure of type III algebras from that. The construction Witten uses in “Gravity and the crossed product,” below.

What has changed since the 1990s

  1. Classification of C*-algebras. The Elliott program for simple nuclear C*-algebras reached its main classification theorem in the 2010s. Strung’s book above is the introduction.
  2. The Connes embedding problem was refuted, by way of Tsirelson’s problem. Zhengfeng Ji, Anand Natarajan, Thomas Vidick, John Wright and Henry Yuen, “MIP* = RE,” Communications of the ACM 64 (2021), no. 11; full version arXiv:2001.04383. The set of quantum correlations obtainable from tensor products of Hilbert spaces is strictly smaller, even after closure, than the set obtainable from commuting operators on a single Hilbert space. That is, the two standard ways of formalizing independent subsystems come apart — a question about the Bell correlations treated in the nonlocality section of the quantum-foundations guide.
  3. Rigidity for type II1 factors. Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory has produced classification results for the factors of groups and group actions. Anantharaman and Popa above is the introduction.
  4. Modular theory in high-energy physics. Since Witten’s 2018 review, Tomita–Takesaki theory and the type classification have become working tools in quantum gravity. See the last section.

Algebraic quantum theory

Ola Bratteli and Derek W. Robinson, Operator Algebras and Quantum Statistical Mechanics (2 vols., 2nd ed., Springer, 1987 and 1997) — reference. Volume 1 is C*- and von Neumann algebras, modular theory and decomposition theory; volume 2 is KMS states, quantum spin systems, and the CCR and CAR algebras.

Rudolf Haag, Local Quantum Physics (2nd ed., Springer, 1996) — textbook. The program set out by its founder.

Huzihiro Araki, Mathematical Theory of Quantum Fields (Oxford, 1999) — textbook. Translated from the Japanese edition of 1993.

Gérard Emch, Algebraic Methods in Statistical Mechanics and Quantum Field Theory (Wiley, 1972; Dover reprint, 2009) — textbook.

Klaas Landsman, Foundations of Quantum Theory: From Classical Concepts to Operator Algebras (Springer, 2017) — reference, free (open access). Appendix C is an introduction to operator algebras. See the entry in the quantum-foundations guide.

Franco Strocchi, An Introduction to the Mathematical Structure of Quantum Mechanics: A Short Course for Mathematicians (2nd ed., World Scientific, Advanced Series in Mathematical Physics 28, 2008) — textbook. About 200 pages. Quantum mechanics formulated from the start in terms of the C*-algebra of observables, motivated by the operational definition of measurement and the duality of states and observables. Schrödinger quantum mechanics then follows from the Heisenberg relations by von Neumann’s uniqueness theorem; the dynamics is treated through self-adjointness of the Hamiltonian, and a final chapter covers the functional-integral approach.

N. P. Landsman, “Lecture notes on C*-algebras, Hilbert C*-modules, and quantum mechanics” (1998) — textbook, free (arXiv:math-ph/9807030). C*-algebras, Hilbert C*-modules, Morita equivalence, induced representations and crossed products, applied to quantization and localization. Assumes elementary functional analysis and quantum mechanics.

Valter Moretti, Fundamental Mathematical Structures of Quantum Theory (Springer, 2019) (doi) — textbook. Lattices of propositions, quantum probability, spectral theory, symmetries, and the basic theory of von Neumann algebras and of the algebraic formulation, with examples and solved exercises; written for physicists and mathematicians alike.

Gabriele Nunzio Tornetta, Mathematical Quantum Physics: A Foundational Introduction (Springer, 2022) (doi) — textbook. About 180 pages. Quantum systems with finitely and infinitely many degrees of freedom, second quantization and scattering, all in operator-algebraic terms, with an account of how algebraic quantum field theory developed.

Miklós Rédei and Stephen J. Summers, “Quantum probability theory,” Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics 38 (2007): 390–417 — survey, free (arXiv:quant-ph/0601158). Projection lattices of von Neumann algebras as non-commutative probability spaces, and the Murray–von Neumann type classification read as a classification of probability theories.

Laura Ruetsche, Interpreting Quantum Theories (Oxford, 2011) — textbook. Inequivalent representations in quantum field theory and quantum statistical mechanics, and what they do to the question of what a theory says.

Stefan Hollands and Ko Sanders, Entanglement Measures and Their Properties in Quantum Field Theory (Springer, SpringerBriefs in Mathematical Physics 34, 2018) (doi) — textbook. Entanglement measures for local algebras, with upper and lower bounds, and the mathematics introduced as needed.

Kasia Rejzner, Perturbative Algebraic Quantum Field Theory (Springer, Mathematical Physics Studies, 2016) — textbook. Perturbative quantum field theory done rigorously in the algebraic framework, without divergent quantities.

Romeo Brunetti, Claudio Dappiaggi, Klaus Fredenhagen and Jakob Yngvason (eds.), Advances in Algebraic Quantum Field Theory (Springer, Mathematical Physics Studies, 2015) — reference.

Measurement

Christopher Fewster and Rainer Verch, “Quantum fields and local measurements,” Communications in Mathematical Physics 378 (2020): 851–889 (doi) — free. One quantum field, the probe, measures another, the system; everything local and covariant, on curved spacetimes. The authors present it as closing a long-open gap between quantum measurement theory and algebraic quantum field theory.

Christopher Fewster, “Lectures on measurement in quantum field theory” (arXiv:2504.17437) and Rainer Verch, “Lecture notes on operator algebras and quantum field theory” (arXiv:2507.00900) — survey, free. Companion courses from the 2025 EMS–IAMP spring school. Verch covers modular theory, the Bisognano–Wichmann and Borchers theorems, locally covariant quantum field theory, and temperature and entropy near black holes.

Quantum gravity and the type of the algebra

Edward Witten, “Gravity and the crossed product,” Journal of High Energy Physics 2022, no. 10: 008 (open access). Leutheusser and Liu found an emergent type III1 algebra in large-N gauge theory; Witten shows that 1/N corrections make it type II∞, the crossed product of the type III1 algebra by its modular automorphism group, and that black-hole entropy is then defined up to an additive constant.

Venkatesa Chandrasekaran, Roberto Longo, Geoff Penington and Edward Witten, “An algebra of observables for de Sitter space,” Journal of High Energy Physics 2023, no. 2: 082 (open access). Operators in a static patch, dressed to an observer’s worldline, form a type II1 algebra; empty de Sitter space is its state of maximum entropy.

Edward Witten, “A background-independent algebra in quantum gravity,” Journal of High Energy Physics 2024, no. 3: 077 (open access). The algebra of operators along an observer’s worldline, proposed as an algebra that does not presuppose a background spacetime.

Hong Liu, “Lectures on entanglement, von Neumann algebras, and emergence of spacetime” — survey, free (arXiv:2510.07017). Expanded from lectures at TASI 2023 and elsewhere. The first half is von Neumann algebras and their classification as a theory of entanglement; the second, the algebraic formulation of AdS/CFT at large N, subregion–subalgebra duality, and gravitational entropy.
