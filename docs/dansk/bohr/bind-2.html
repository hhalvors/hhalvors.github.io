Atomfysik og menneskelig erkendelse / Essays 1932–1957 on Atomic Physics and Human Knowledge

Twenty-five years of occasional papers, from Light and Life (1932) to the pieces written for the collection itself in 1957. It contains the Discussion with Einstein, the fullest account Bohr gave of the argument that occupied him for two decades, and the essays extending the epistemological lesson to biology and to human cultures.

The text

Eight volumes are planned, four Danish and four English; this is volume II of each. The Danish follows Filosofiske Skrifter, Bind II (Forlaget Philosophia, København 2013, with the Niels Bohr Arkiv); the English follows Woodbridge, Conn.: Ox Bow Press, 1987. Reprint of Atomic Physics and Human Knowledge, John Wiley & Sons, New York 1958.

Editorial note

This edition’s one substantive addition to the existing printings is dual marginal pagination. Bohr is normally cited from the collected volumes — “PWNB II, p. 74” — but the arguments were first made in journals, congress proceedings and Festschriften, and it is that pagination the contemporary correspondence and the Niels Bohr Collected Works cite. A reader working between the two has had to convert by hand. Here both are marked:

  • [n], bold, in the left margin — page n of the original publication named in that essay’s First published line above.
  • |n|, italic, in the right margin — page n of the collected volume: Ox Bow Press in English, Philosophia in Danish.

Markers sit at the exact word the page turns on. They are set with marginnote rather than \marginpar so that they survive inside block quotations, lists and footnotes, where \marginpar silently drops them. Because the collected editions reset the type, the two series interleave irregularly; that is the point.

Transcription follows the house rule for this repository: the text is diplomatic. Printer’s errors are set as printed and logged in a % comment at the site, never silently corrected.

Rights

Unlike everything else in this collection, these texts are not public domain. Bohr died in 1962; the Danish edition (Philosophia 2013) and the English (Ox Bow Press) are both in copyright. Permission to publish has been obtained, and this volume is live on the site. The permission covers the eight volumes described here; further Bohr material — correspondence, the Collected Works, the unpublished manuscripts in the Niels Bohr Arkiv — is not covered by it and should not be added without checking.
