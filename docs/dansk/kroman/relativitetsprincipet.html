Hans Halvorson

»Relativitetsprincipet« (1915)

In 1915 a sixty-nine-year-old professor of philosophy at Copenhagen published thirty pages in Fysisk Tidsskrift arguing that Einstein’s special theory of relativity contradicts the principle of non-contradiction and must therefore be rejected. The argument is short enough to state in a paragraph, and Carl Henrik Koch quotes its core:

Her er altsaa to Sandheder. A har Ret; thi han har ingen Acceleration. Og B har ogsaa Ret; thi han har heller ingen Acceleration. Men dette gaar aabenbart ikke an. Der er i Logikken en Grundsætning, der siger: A er ikke Ikke-A; Modsigelser (i Tingenes Verden) findes ikke, og Modsigelser (i Tankens Verden) taales ikke. Sandheden er ikke dobbelt. Sandheden er kun én. Denne Sætning trænger ikke til Forsvar. Enhver Videnskab er bygget over den. Einstein’s Teori staar derimod i Strid med den. Derfor maa den forkastes.

So here are two truths. A is right, for he has no acceleration. And B is right too, for he has no acceleration either. But this plainly will not do. There is a principle in logic that says: A is not not-A; contradictions (in the world of things) do not exist, and contradictions (in the world of thought) are not tolerated. Truth is not double. Truth is only one. This proposition needs no defence. Every science is built upon it. Einstein’s theory, by contrast, stands in conflict with it. Therefore it must be rejected.

Two observers in uniform relative motion each measure the other’s rod as the shorter. Neither is accelerating, so neither can be demoted to the merely apparent case. Kroman takes this to be a straight contradiction, and takes the law of non-contradiction to be non-negotiable.

The objection is not eccentric — the same move was made across the international discussion of the period — and Koch notes that Kroman’s papers show he had full command of the (after all fairly simple) mathematics of special relativity. What makes the episode worth preserving is that in Denmark it was a philosopher who opened the argument, that a physicist and a mathematician answered him in a physics journal, and that the reply which finally settled the matter for Danish philosophy came from Harald Høffding, in a book this collection already carries in both languages.

The premise doing the work is not local to relativity. Vor Naturerkjendelse (1883) grounds the certainty of the formal sciences in necessity of intuition — »Al Tankenødvendighed beror til syvende og sidst paa Anskuelsesnødvendighed« — and on that ground had already dismissed the non-Euclidean geometries as contentless fantasies. Dansk Biografisk Leksikon names this single premise as the source of both polemics. Kroman returned to the geometry side of it in Matematikken og Erkendelseslæren (1920). The relativity articles are best read as one application of a thirty-year-old commitment, not as a late aberration.

The debate

With the volumes in hand the exchange turns out to run to seven, and the extra two are not in any bibliography consulted: a second piece by Holst, and a mediating article by Carl Emil Walsøe. Hansen’s reply is at pp. 18–29, not the pp. 14–22 previously inferred from search hits.

Kroman’s own closing article confirms the shape. At printed p. 193 he writes »Med Forfatteren af den tredie Afhandling, Hr. Dr. H. M. Hansen, er jeg derimod erkendelsesteoretisk set meget uenig« — with the author of the third treatise, Dr. H. M. Hansen, I am on the other hand, epistemologically speaking, in strong disagreement. Three treatises answered him, and Hansen wrote the third: items 3, 4 and 5, in that order.

Three of these articles begin partway down the last page of the one before, under the previous article’s running head — which is why the running heads give the wrong extent every time and why three of the seven entries in this table had to be corrected against the pages. Walsøe shares p. 13 with the end of Holst’s »Tidsproblemet« (catalogued 14–17, actually 13–17); Holst’s second piece begins in the lower half of p. 165 (catalogued 166–168, actually 165–168); and Kroman’s 1917 reply begins partway down p. 192, under P. O. Pedersen’s running head. In this journal, always read the page before the apparent first page.

Walsøe belongs to the debate rather than merely sitting between its parts. His p. 17 describes itself as »denne Antydning fra Trediemand af en Mulighed for […] at udløse Spændingen og bilægge Striden mellem de to Parter« — this suggestion, from a third party, of a way to release the tension and settle the dispute between the two parties. He is trying to mediate between Kroman and the physicists. Hansen’s own opening at p. 18 registers the same pile-up: »Den Interesse for Relativitetsspørgsmaalet, som flere Artikler i Fysisk Tidsskrift i den senere Tid har givet Vidnesbyrd om…«

Hansen’s 1912 lecture is the first real introduction of special relativity to a Danish audience, and it is what provoked Kroman three years later. H. M. Hansen is Hans Marius Hansen, Bohr’s collaborator; that Bohr had also sat Kroman’s filosofikum makes the exchange a small collision of two parts of the same university. Koch’s summary of the ending is that Hansen answered the year after the attack, that a couple of further contributions followed, and that Kroman closed the debate without adding anything new. From about 1920 — the year Einstein visited Copenhagen, and the year after Eddington — Danish physicists appear simply to have accepted the theory.

Item 1 is now the only gap: Google’s run of the journal skips vols 10–12, so Hansen’s 1912 lecture has to be ordered from KB. Everything else is in hand.

Høffding’s reply

Relation som Kategori (1921), §3d.δ »Den nye Relativitetsteori«, pp. 72–77, answers Kroman by name — the only place in this collection where two of its authors argue directly with each other about physics. Our transcription carries the passage at printed pp. 75–76:

Man har overfor den Einsteinske Hypotese […] gjort gældende, at der dog kun kan være én Sandhed i hvert enkelt Tilfælde, og at det vilde stride mod Modsigelsens Grundsætning, hvis to Iagttagere, af hvilke den ene erklærer for samtidigt, hvad den anden erklærer for successivt, begge skulde have Ret.

with the footnote »Kroman i Tidsskrift for Fysik, XIV, p. 15.« Høffding’s answer is that there is no reason to fear for the unity of truth even if Einstein turns out to be right:

Den egentlige Sandhed ligger nemlig da i den Nødvendighed, der fysisk paavises af, at forskellige Iagttagere under visse bestemte Betingelser faar forskellig Tidsopfattelse.

The real truth lies in this case in the necessity, physically demonstrated, that different observers under certain determinate conditions arrive at different apprehensions of time. An observer who is also a physicist can explain why another observer apprehends time differently; the disagreement is itself lawful, and the law is the truth. Høffding files this under the same heading as Copernicus — a further instance of the lesson that the observer’s own standpoint must enter the account — and adds that if Einstein is right one does not therefore have to philosophise in a new way. He took the point up again, without pressing it, in Erkendelsesteori og Livsopfattelse (1925).

Note that Høffding gets the journal’s name backwards. There is no Tidsskrift for Fysik; the journal is Fysisk Tidsskrift. The volume and page are right — XIV, p. 15 falls inside Kroman’s pp. 1–30 — and the error is reproduced in at least one modern bibliography, so it has had a career of its own. Anyone searching a library catalogue on Høffding’s form of the title will find nothing.

Where the text is

Google Books Tr4ZAAAAIAAJ — Fysisk tidsskrift, volumes 13–15 bound together, the University of California copy, digitised 17 January 2007, in full view with a PDF download. One file holds Kroman’s 1915 article, Holst’s »Tidsproblemet«, Kroman’s 1917 reply and, on the evidence of the volume range, Hansen’s reply as well. Verified by searching inside it: p. 192 opens with the heading »Et Par yderligere Bemærkninger om »Relativitetsprincipet««. Hansen’s 1912 lecture is in vol. 10 and will be in one of the other Google volumes of the journal, which carry edition dates 1910, 1918, 1928 and 1932.

It is a US library copy of a Danish journal, set in Antiqua rather than Fraktur, so the Google text layer will be better than usual — but re-OCR per TRANSCRIPTION-PLAYBOOK.md §2 before trusting it.

Why the Royal Library could not find it

Two attempts to have KB digitise this failed, and the reason is in the records rather than in the library. What KB catalogues under Kroman is not the article but the offprint, and the offprints live in the Særtrykssamlingen, which is filed by author and has no running call number. Both records give the shelfmark as the bare string Særtrykssamlingen 8°. The 1917 record adds U. titelbl. — uden titelblad, no title page. So the request, as it reaches the stacks, asks someone to find an untitled fourteen-page pamphlet in an author-filed collection with no unique locator. Add Høffding’s inverted journal name, if it travelled with the request, and failure is the expected outcome.

The general rule, which applies well beyond this item: when a KB record says Særtrykssamlingen, do not order the offprint. Order the journal volume named in the record’s »Særtr. af:« field, or find the journal elsewhere. In this case the journal was on Google Books all along.

KB record for the 1915 offprint: alma99122230022205763, 30 s., »Særtryk af: Fysisk Tidsskrift XIV. 1. Hefte«. For the 1917 offprint: alma99122363662605763, 14 s., »Særtr. af: Fysisk Tidsskrift«. The page counts match the article page ranges exactly, 30 and 14, which is what confirms the citations above.

Transcribing the debate

Six of the seven items are in the Google file and the page map is now measured, not guessed. pagemap.py --detect in each article directory reports the offset; the three settled so far are

Kroman 1915   printed   1-30   = PDF 331-360   (offset 330)
Holst 1916    printed   1-13   = PDF 603-615   (offset 602)
Kroman 1917   printed 192-205  = PDF 814-827   (offset 622)

The numerals alone do not identify an article in this bind. Each of vols 13, 14 and 15 has its own pp. 1-30, so the detector finds three offsets that fit Kroman 1915’s numerals perfectly – 12, 330 and 602. What breaks the tie is reading the pages: PDF 331 opens »Relativitetsprincipet«. / Af / K. Kroman., and PDF 345 carries »Sandheden er ikke dobbelt«, which is printed p. 15 and so exactly the page Høffding’s footnote cites. Offset 602 turned out to be Holst’s article, which is how item 3’s page map got settled in the same pass. The detector now prints every tied candidate rather than choosing one silently.

Note also that the offset is not constant across vol. 15: it is 602 at the front and 622 by p. 192, because unpaginated advertisement leaves are bound in (PDF 795 is an ad for H. Struers kemiske Laboratorium). Each article’s offset must be measured for that article. Never carry one across.

Item 1 is the gap: Google’s run of Fysisk Tidsskrift is vols 7–9, 13–15, 16–18, 26–27 and 30–31 — vols 10–12 are missing, and Danmarks Tekniske Kulturarv has the journal listed as ikke digitaliseret. But unlike the Kroman offprints, Hansen’s 1912 offprint has a real call number: KB record alma99122809209105763, 8° Fys. 11882 at Danmarks Natur- og Lægevidenskabelige Bibliotek, »Særtr. af Fys. Tidskr. 10. 6. Hefte«. That one can simply be ordered. (A second copy sits in the Særtrykssamlingen as alma99122564603005763, S. 251–281 — do not order that one.)

Two practical notes for whoever starts. The scan is Antiqua, not Fraktur, so the Fraktur model is the wrong tool and the Google text layer will be better than usual — but still re-OCR per TRANSCRIPTION-PLAYBOOK.md §2. And this is the first material in the collection with real mathematics in it: Lorentz transformations, coordinate systems, and Kroman’s own algebra. The existing harness has never had to carry display formulas, so budget for that rather than discovering it at batch three.

Copyright — not all of this can be published yet

The collection’s usual assumption, that anything 19th-century is long out of copyright, does not hold here. Danish term is life plus seventy.

Author Died Public domain in Denmark
K. Kroman 26 July 1925 since 1996 — clear
Helge Holst 1944 since 2015 — clear
H. M. Hansen 13 June 1956 1 January 2027
Carl Emil Walsøe 1951 since 2022 — clear

So items 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 can be transcribed and published now. Only items 1 and 5, Hansen’s two contributions, are still in copyright, and only until the end of this year.

Walsøe took some finding, because the article is a dead end: the byline is bare »Af / C. E. Walsøe.«, with no title, affiliation, town or dateline anywhere, and the *) on its title turns out to be the Redaktion’s own disclaimer that the piece »maa siges at falde uden for Tidsskriftets Ramme« — not a note on the author. He is Carl Emil Walsøe, b. 25 May 1870 in Copenhagen, d. 1951: cand. polyt. 1896, engineering assistant at the State Railways, telegraph engineer from 1903, district engineer at Post- og Telegrafvæsenet 1926–40. Kraks Blå Bog 1949 has the birth date and career; his son Sven’s 1957 entry supplies the death year. What makes this more than a match on initials is the list of his own writings in the 1949 entry — »Den ny Naturvidenskab og vor gamle Kristentro. Et Forsøg paa en principiel Orientering« (1938) and »De tre Planer. Legeme, Sjæl, Aand.« (1947). A polytechnically trained engineer writing on the boundary between the new physics and religious conviction is exactly the author of a 1916 piece called »Omkring Videnskabens Grænse«, and exactly the contributor a physics journal’s editors would disclaim. Transcribing them now is fine; publishing them is not, and the debate can go up complete at the new year. Worth deciding deliberately, since it is also the first time this collection would carry a 20th-century text whose author’s term has not run.

Open questions

  • Does Kroman answer Høffding? Høffding’s reply is 1921 and Kroman lived to 1925, but the Fysisk Tidsskrift debate had closed in 1917. Whether he responded anywhere — the 1920 Matematikken og Erkendelseslæren is the obvious place to look — is unsettled.
  • Jørgen Jørgensen in Spectator I (1916), pp. 384–387. Listed in the DBL bibliography for Kroman and dated to the middle of the debate. Very possibly a sixth item in it; not yet seen.
  • Émile Meyerson cites Kroman in Identité et réalité (1908) and in De l’explication dans les sciences (1921). Høffding’s correspondence with Meyerson is already in this catalog, so there may be a line running from Kroman through Høffding to Meyerson worth following.
  • Koch, »Kristian Kroman«, Danish Yearbook of Philosophy XIII (1976), pp. 147–166, is the standard scholarly treatment and has not been consulted. The account above leans on Koch’s popular article in Uniavisen instead.
