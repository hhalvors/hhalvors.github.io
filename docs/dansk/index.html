A note on the word “Danish”. Denmark and Norway were joined under a single crown from 1380 and administered as one state from the sixteenth century until 1814, governed from Copenhagen — which held the only university of the joint realms until the Royal Frederick University was founded in Christiania in 1811. Danish was the written language of educated Norwegians no less than of Danes. Ludvig Holberg, born in Bergen, and Niels Treschow, born in Drammen, are accordingly claimed today by Norway as well as by Denmark; but neither faced a choice between two national literatures, and for their own lifetimes the question does not arise. Treschow is the exact hinge: he held the chair of philosophy at Copenhagen until 1813, moved to the new university in Christiania, and saw the union dissolved by the Treaty of Kiel in January 1814. “Danish” here therefore means the philosophy of that shared realm and its shared written language, rather than of the post-1814 nation-state.

Ludvig Holberg (1684–1754)

Bergen-born, Copenhagen-based playwright, historian, essayist and professor — the founder of modern Danish-Norwegian literature and, for Høffding, the founder of independent philosophical reflection in Denmark: »Danske Filosofer« (1909) opens with him. Holberg is no system-builder but a skeptic and empiricist in the Montaigne–Bayle vein, hostile to dogma and speculative systems, insisting that "man må først lære at tvivle førend man må lære at tro" (one must first learn to doubt before one can learn to believe). His relevance to the line running toward Bohr's "epistemological lesson" is one of disposition rather than doctrine: epistemic humility, the testing of received norms against sound reason, and — especially in »Niels Klim« — a sustained play with the relativity of standpoint, the recognition that how things appear depends on the observer's position. This anti-dogmatic, perspective-conscious temper is the seedbed of the critical Danish tradition that Høffding inherits and transmits. Further works not listed below include »Introduction til Naturens og Folke-Rettens Kundskab« (1716), the »Moralske Fabler« (1751), and the Latin autobiographical epistles (1728–1743).

Moralske Tanker
1744. Kjøbenhavn
Holberg's central philosophical work: a collection of reflective essays, framed as commentaries on his own epigrams, on morality, religion, knowledge and doubt. The clearest statement of his anti-systematic skepticism and the "doubt before belief" method that Høffding takes as the starting point of Danish philosophy.

Satirical-philosophical subterranean voyage in Latin. Niels Treschow (1751–1833)

Norwegian-born philosopher; professor of philosophy at Copenhagen 1803–1813, then the first professor of philosophy at the new Frederik University in Christiania (Oslo) from 1813. A Spinoza- and Leibniz-influenced monist and self-described Neoplatonist whose late "Identitets-system" treats the mental and the bodily — and, more broadly, the ideal and the real, subject and object — as two sides of one reality. His insistence that "in the real world only individuals exist" (universals being mere "Hjælpemidler til Oversyn," aids to survey, not knowledge of things in themselves) makes him an early source of the emphasis on concrete individuality that runs through nineteenth-century Danish philosophy. The relevance to the Bohr line is direct: Harald Høffding — Bohr's philosophical mentor, and himself in this catalog — discussed Treschow at length in »Danske Filosofer« (1909) and reprinted his essay on individuality in »Udvalgte Stykker af dansk filosofisk Litteratur« (1910). Treschow is thus the earliest figure on the Danish subject–object / individuality tradition that reaches Bohr's "epistemological lesson." The philosophical books are all listed below. Further items not catalogued here are the pre-1794 juvenilia and occasional pieces (»Om Veltalenheds Nytte« 1776, »Om offentlig Underviisning i Videnskaber« 1785, the 1790 dissertation »De anthropomorphismo«), the 1807 »Anatomisk-physiologiske Forelæsninger for Anthropologer«, the 1816 prize essay on rights and moral law, the 1817 »Politisk Catechismus«, and the 1830 verse »Skabelsen, Mennesket og Messias«. All are digitised and public-domain at nb.no.

Forsøg om Guds Tilværelse af theoretiske Grunde: i Anledning af den kantiske Philosophie
1794. Kjøbenhavn
The earlier of Treschow's two early critiques of Kant (1794, 1798), written directly »i Anledning af den kantiske Philosophie«: a defence of theoretical grounds for God's existence against Kant's verdict that the speculative proofs fail and that God can be reached only through practical reason. His earliest published engagement with critical philosophy and the starting point of the trajectory that leads to the monist »Identitets-system« of the 1831–33 »Testament«.

Forelæsninger over den Kantiske Philosophie
1798. Kjøbenhavn: Gyldendals Forlag
The later of the two early critiques of Kant, four years after the »Forsøg om Guds Tilværelse« and on a much larger scale (361 printed pp.): a course of lectures expounding and assessing the critical philosophy as a whole rather than contesting a single doctrine. A German version, »Vorlesungen über die Kantische Philosophie. Erster Theil« (421 pp.), appeared the same year and is separately digitised; worth establishing whether it is a translation of this volume or an independent recasting, and whether a second German part ever followed. Together with the 1794 »Forsøg« this is the record of what Treschow took from Kant and what he refused, and so the baseline against which the monism of the 1805 »Philosophiske Forsøg« should be read. Kort Udkast til Kundskab om Mennesket, eller Anthropologie til Brug for de lærde Skoler
1803. Kjøbenhavn: Fr. Brummers Forlag, hos Sebastian Popp
A school textbook of anthropology, written for the »lærde Skoler« in the year Treschow took the Copenhagen chair. It is the direct predecessor of the 1812 »Om den menneskelige Natur«, which is described in this catalog as the first psychology written in Danish; the interest of the 1803 book is therefore what the doctrine of the twofold — bodily and mental — access to the human being looked like nine years earlier, and in a form pitched at schoolboys. Gives der noget Begreb eller nogen Idee om enslige Ting? Besvaret med Hensyn til Menneskeværd og Menneskevel
1810. Det Kgl. Danske Videnskabernes Selskabs Skrifter, bd. 5
Short, self-contained treatise arguing that only individuals are real and that universals are mere aids to survey, not exact knowledge of things in themselves. The core statement of Treschow's primacy of the individual — the theme most resonant with Bohr's language of the "individuality" (indivisibility) of the quantum phenomenon. Reprinted in excerpt by Høffding, »Udvalgte Stykker af dansk filosofisk Litteratur« (1910), and complete in A.H. Winsnes' ed. of »Philosophiske Forsøg og andre Skrifter« (1966). Jørgen Juul Nielsen) — see `modern-editions` below, and note that the retitling is why it hides in library catalogues. NB: the essay is signed 1807 and printed in the volume published 1810; it is set in antiqua, not Fraktur, and uses »ö« rather than »ø« throughout. The key formulation is on printed p. 240: general concepts are »kun Hielpemidler til Oversyn, ei til nöiagtig Kundskab om Tingene i sig selv«; the thesis is restated on p. 247: »Der gives altsaa et fast Begreb om enslige Ting.«

Moral for Folk og Stat
1810. Kjøbenhavn: Fr. Brummers Forlag, hos J. F. Schultz
Treschow's moral philosophy, published the same year as the »enslige Ting« essay and running to 481 printed pp. The pairing of »Folk« and »Stat« in the title is the point of interest here: it is the first place to look for how far the primacy of the individual extends from metaphysics into ethics and politics, which is the question Brekke raises (see secondary literature below) and which »Lovgivnings-Principier« then takes up at length. Om Philosophiens Natur og Dele: en dogmatisk og historisk Indledning til denne Videnskab
1811. Kjøbenhavn
A metaphilosophical introduction — what philosophy is, how it divides into parts, and how it got that way — expanding the 1805 »Indledning til den hele Philosophie«. Om den menneskelige Natur i Almindelighed, især dens aandelige Sider
1812. Kjøbenhavn
The first psychology written in Danish, and the fullest statement of Treschow's double-aspect view. Man is not a composite of soul and body; the mental and the bodily are the same thing considered as an object of the inner and the outer sense. The programme is stated in § 2 — the human being »kan betragtes fra en dobbelt Side, nemlig en legemlig og aandelig«, with no »fælles Sands« in the middle from which both could be seen united — and made precise in the 2det Hovedstykke § 12 (p. 79), »Sandselighedens første Lov«: every bodily motion is bound to a mental sensation answering to it in quality and magnitude, and conversely, yet »Forholdet mellem begge er altsaa ikke caussalt«. A strict parallelism, and the 1812 anticipation of the identity system of »Om Gud«. Two further passages tie the book to the 1810 prize essay: p. 168, where individuality is itself »Tingenes uforanderlige Grundform« and »Enhver enslig Ting kan i denne Mening betragtes som Aftryk af en Idee, ei Arterne og Slægterne alene«; and p. 151, where the Linnaean system, horticulture and forestry classify plants by different marks yet »i alle tre Videnskaber kan Forstanden vise Grundighed«, the pure sciences aiming at Tingenes Væsen and the applied at Vigtighed for a purpose. Body: printed pp. 1–486, in an Indledning and four Hovedstykker (Forestillingsevnen, Føleevnen, Villien). Transcription of the complete work is under way and image-verified page by page.

Almindelig Logik
1813. Kjøbenhavn: Fr. Brummers Forlag
His textbook of logic / theory of knowledge, the counterpart to the 1812 psychology; develops the epistemology of the individual (concepts of particulars vs. general concepts). §§ 26–34 supply the machinery the 1810 essay presupposes but never states. The key passage is § 30 (printed p. 97): »Slægter saavelsom Arter ere deels egentlige, deels uegentlige eller analogiske. Hines Kiendemærker ere væsentlige og bestandige, disses blot tilfældige og ubestandige« — a criterion of naturalness for classifications. Treschow does not discard the improper kinds: they are »nødvendige« where no proper kind is to be had, whence the »kunstige Systemer«. §§ 32–34 give a theory of division (partitio vs. divisio, the ground of a division, and the rules that the parts must exhaust the whole, be mutually exclusive, and admit no leap from higher to lower species); and § 35 turns § 30 into a rule of method — take the ground of a division from things' essential and abiding criteria, and where none are to be had, choose those that come *nearest* to them, which makes naturalness graded rather than binary. NB § 31 denies that the highest being is a whole of which individuals are parts — pantheism and the emanation- system are said to rest on that confusion. Fraktur; uses »ø«, unlike the antiqua 1810 essay. 330 printed pp. in all; only §§ 26–35 are transcribed here — they close the First Main Part (p. 108).

Treschow's summa, written in retirement and prefaced with an autobiography; the detailed statement of his monist "Identitets-system." God is not the sum of things but "a real individual" that makes all things one; consciousness "pictures the Absolute One," standing to the manifold as the mind stands to its own thoughts and decisions. The most explicit source for the subject–object identity theme. NB: the third word is »Sandse-« (world of sense), not »Sandheds-«. Two spans of vol. I are transcribed here because they settle a problem raised by »Almindelig Logik« § 31, which denies that the highest being is »noget Heelt, som Verden, hvoraf alle Individuer ere Dele«. The answer (p. 220) is that the unity of the ideal world is constituted by »Vexelvirkning« among Ideas that do not alter one another — a relational, not a mereological unity — and that (p. 221) »Disse ere de Individuer, hvoraf Sandseverdenen bestaaer«: individuals compose the world of sense, not God. p. 207 gives the sharpest statement of the primacy of the individual anywhere in the corpus: all class-, genus- and species-concepts are »mere eller mindre vaklende og ubestemmelige, men Individuerne i sig selv saa fuldkommen bestemte«, while an individual may pass over into another species, genus, order or class. p. 206 marks the break with Plato: the Ideas are individual, not the objects of general concepts.

Treschow on Dannelse (Bildung) and gender. Brekke's case is that although Treschow held that women should be excluded from the university — importing and continuing a Continental ideal of Dannelse that did so — he nonetheless works out a more nuanced model of gender than the period's dominant one: against academia as a homosocial, over-rationalised and professionalised sphere, he grounds both individual and social Dannelse in sociability, the family, and feminine models of sympathy and care. Hans Christian Ørsted (1777–1851)

Physicist, chemist and natural philosopher; professor at Copenhagen from 1806 and founder of the Polytekniske Læreanstalt (1829). In 1820 he discovered electromagnetism — that an electric current deflects a magnetic needle — and in 1825 was the first to isolate metallic aluminium. But Ørsted was as much a philosopher of nature as an experimenter: a student of Kant and of the German Naturphilosophie, he held that the same reason the mind finds in itself is objectively at work in nature, so that the laws of thought and the laws of nature ultimately coincide. This dynamical monism — opposed forces as convertible expressions of one underlying activity — is the thesis of his late essay-collection »Aanden i Naturen« (1850), "the spirit (reason) in nature," and it is why he belongs in this catalog: it is a Danish source of the unity-of-nature theme running toward Bohr. Ørsted also gave physics the term »Tankeeksperiment« (thought experiment) and coined much of the modern Danish scientific vocabulary; Høffding treated him among the »Danske Filosofer«. Two English editions cover most of his important work: »The Soul in Nature« (trans. L. & J. B. Horner, London: Bohn, 1852) for the philosophy of nature, and the 83-item »Selected Scientific Works of Hans Christian Ørsted« (trans. Jelved, Jackson & Knudsen, Princeton, 1998) for the scientific and aesthetic essays — the source of most of the translation links below. Further works not listed below include »Videnskaben om Naturens almindelige Love« (1809), »Naturlærens mechaniske Deel« (1844), and the poem »Luftskibet« (1836).

J.L. Heiberg (1791–1860)

Johan Ludvig Heiberg — poet, vaudeville dramatist, critic, editor, from 1849 director of the Royal Theatre, and the man who made Hegel a Danish subject. He is in this catalog for one reason: almost every philosophical quarrel the other authors here are having is a quarrel he started. In Berlin in 1824 he met Hegel, attended his lectures, and underwent what he later described as a philosophical conversion; the same year he brought out »Om den menneskelige Frihed« from Kiel, the first work in Danish literature to argue a case on Hegelian ground. From then until the early 1840s he ran what was in effect a campaign — through his journals »Kjøbenhavns flyvende Post« (1827–37), »Perseus« (1837–38) and »Intelligensblade« (1842–44), through the logic textbook written for his cadets at the Royal Military College, and through the public lecture series announced by »Om Philosophiens Betydning for den nuværende Tid« (1833) — to persuade Golden Age Denmark that Hegel's system was the cure for an age he diagnosed as being in crisis. The campaign is the hinge of this catalog's middle. Martensen, who reviewed the »Indlednings-Foredrag« in 1836, took the speculative program into theology and became the target of both Kierkegaard and Nielsen. Sibbern's »Bemærkninger og Undersøgelser, fornemmelig betreffende Hegels Philosophie« (1838) — transcribed and translated in full under Sibbern above — is formally a review of the first volume of »Perseus«, and remains the most thorough Danish critique of Hegel ever written. Poul Martin Møller's late turn against "the modern atheism" is a turn against this same speculative fashion. And Heiberg's slogan »Tidens Fordring«, the demand of the age, together with the promise that philosophy can »gaae videre«, is what Kierkegaard spends the pseudonymous authorship mocking, in »Forord« most directly, but also throughout the »Efterskrift«. Heiberg is thus the positive term that Sibbern, Møller and Kierkegaard are all negating, and the source from which the faith-and-knowledge controversy running through Nielsen and Brøchner ultimately flows. He is unusually well served in English: Jon Stewart has translated the whole philosophical corpus in the first six volumes of »Texts from Golden Age Denmark«, so translation here is low priority and the useful work is Danish transcription. Not catalogued below are the 1825 essay on contingency and its 1827 sequel on Nemesis (Stewart's volume 4), the aesthetic and theatre writings (»Om Vaudevillen« 1826, »Om Theatret«), the astronomical-poetic yearbook »Urania« (1844–46), and the verse — above all »En Sjæl efter Døden« in »Nye Digte« (1841), a Hegelian satire in dramatic form and the most read thing he wrote.

Om den menneskelige Frihed. I Anledning af de nyeste Stridigheder over denne Gjenstand
1824. Kiel: i Universitets-Boghandlingen; 104 pp.
Heiberg's first philosophical publication and, by common consent, the first Hegelian work in Danish. Written in Kiel, where he lectured on Danish language and literature 1822–25, and published in the year of his meeting with Hegel in Berlin. It is an intervention in the »Howitz-fejde«, the dispute over freedom of the will touched off by the physician F.G. Howitz's determinism and answered by A.S. Ørsted, J.P. Mynster and P. Hjort: Heiberg's move is to argue that the opposition between freedom and necessity dissolves at the standpoint of the absolute — the pattern of every argument he would make for the next twenty years. Grundtræk til Philosophiens Philosophie eller den speculative Logik. Som Ledetraad ved Forelæsninger paa den kongelige militaire Høiskole
1832. Kjøbenhavn: Andreas Seidelin; 126, [2] pp.
Heiberg's most extensive work on logic, written as a textbook for his students at the Royal Military College (den kongelige militaire Høiskole). NB the title: it is »Grundtræk«, not »Grundlinier« — the latter belongs to a quite different, German, 1824 piece on aesthetics (»Grundlinien zum Systeme der Aesthetik«, re-edited by Klaus Müller-Wille in 2022), and the two are easy to confuse. KB's record also identifies this printing as a revised setting of an earlier »Ledetraad ved Forelæsningerne over Philosophiens Philosophie eller den speculative Logik ved den kongelige militaire Høiskole« (1831–32), of which KB holds a copy at Fil. 54495 4°; a transcription should say which of the two it follows and ideally collate them. The book follows Hegel's »Wissenschaft der Logik« closely, and is the first of the series of Danish works on Hegel's logic that runs through the late 1830s and 1840s — Nielsen's and Brøchner's logical writings, catalogued below, are downstream of it. The themes it puts into Danish circulation are exactly the ones the later controversy turns on: the presuppositionless beginning, mediation, the law of excluded middle, and the relation of philosophy to religion. Om Philosophiens Betydning for den nuværende Tid. Et Indbydelses-Skrift til en Række af philosophiske Forelæsninger
1833. Kjøbenhavn: C.A. Reitzel; 55 pp.
The manifesto of Danish Hegelianism, and the most consequential thing Heiberg wrote as a philosopher: an invitation to a lecture series, which turns into a diagnosis of the age as one of relativism and dissolution — art, religion and scholarship all having lost their ground — and a prescription that thinkers take refuge in speculative philosophy, which alone can restore truth and beauty to their places. Its claims about the standing of religion drew fire from Copenhagen's theologians, Mynster among them, and Heiberg answered; Stewart's volume collects the whole exchange. 55 printed pages, which makes it the obvious first transcription for this author. Indlednings-Foredrag til det i November 1834 begyndte logiske Cursus paa den kongelige militaire Høiskole
1835. Kjøbenhavn: Schubothe; 42 pp., Fraktur
Delivered as a lecture in 1834 and printed the following year; the clearest single statement of Heiberg's Hegelian idealism. Arguing from a theory of categories, he places philosophical knowing above both religious knowing and the natural sciences — the claim that made the piece a scandal — and, despite the title, ranges well beyond logic into philosophy of language and a taxonomy of poetic forms. It is here that the appeal to »Tidens Fordring«, the demand of the age, is made in the form Kierkegaard would satirise in »Forord« and »Stadier paa Livets Vei«. Martensen's review of it (1836) is his own entry into the debate, and Sibbern and Nielsen followed; the resulting exchange, on the relation of philosophy to religion, is the direct ancestor of the Nielsen–Brøchner controversy catalogued below. Perseus, Journal for den speculative Idee, Nr. 1–2
1837–1838. Kjøbenhavn: C.A. Reitzel
Heiberg's philosophical journal, founded in June 1837 after the editors of »Maanedsskrift for Litteratur« declined an article of his and he withdrew it in protest. Two issues only, but the landmark of the Danish Hegel reception: the prefatory address calls on Perseus to champion speculative idealism against the Medusa of realism and empiricism, and the second issue carries »Det logiske System«, the fragment of Heiberg's own system that got as far as §23 and stopped. The journal is the occasion of two of the most important texts in this catalog — Sibbern's »Bemærkninger og Undersøgelser« (1838), which began as an eight-part review of Nr. 1 and is transcribed complete under Sibbern, and Kierkegaard's »Forord« (1844), which satirises it. Forberedelser til en Afhandling om Affectation
1837. Efterladte Skrifter, bd. 3, ed. F.C. Olsen, C.A. Reitzel
Fragment (Brudstykke IV): materials toward a projected treatise on affectation — one of Møller's signature ethical-psychological concepts (inauthenticity, self-deception). Opens with a connected treatment (begun 1837) defining affectation as a compound of falsity and self-deception and distinguishing three degrees (momentary, fixed, changing); then the editor's thematic grouping of the author's scattered aphoristic notes, rich in cultural diagnosis. Contains the striking "two sides" passage — "Da enhver Sag kan sees fra to Sider, var det tilfældigt, fra hvilken Side den øieblikkeligen blev seet" — and the China frog-bundle image. Pairs directly with § 29 of the Moralphilosophi lectures. Transcribed from the Fraktur setting of Efterladte Skrifter vol. 3 (3rd ed.), printed pp. 163–188.

Om Begrebet Ironie
1835–1837. Efterladte Skrifter, bd. 3, ed. F.C. Olsen, C.A. Reitzel
Fragment (Brudstykke II) occasioned by Provost Tryde's review of Sibbern's Æsthetik (Maanedsskrift for Litteratur, bd. 13, 1835). As a member of the journal's editorial board Møller began appended remarks on the concept of irony but, per the editor's note, "bragte kun Indledningen til Ende": only the treatment of MORAL irony was completed, and the piece breaks off as it turns to poetic irony ("Vi gaae nu over til den poetiske Ironie"). Hans Lassen Martensen (1808–1884)

Theologian: Privatdocent and from 1840 professor of theology at Copenhagen, court preacher from 1845, and Mynster's successor as Bishop of Zealand from 1854. Usually classified as a theologian rather than a philosopher, but a necessary part of this catalog's story, because he is the man both Kierkegaard and Nielsen wrote against. His lectures on speculative dogmatics from the late 1830s made Hegelian theology the reigning fashion in Copenhagen — the mediation of faith and knowledge, and the ambition to »gaae videre«, to go further than Descartes' doubt and Hegel's system — and it is these commonplaces that Kierkegaard's unfinished »Johannes Climacus eller De omnibus dubitandum est« and the polemic against speculation in the »Efterskrift« (1846) satirise: the Climacus pseudonym is aimed at a Martensenian target. When the »Christelige Dogmatik« finally appeared in 1849, Nielsen's »undersøgende Anmeldelse« of it (the »johannesclimacos« entry below) opened the faith/knowledge controversy that runs through this catalog from Nielsen to Brøchner to Høffding; Martensen answered once, in the »Dogmatiske Oplysninger« (1850), and thereafter left the defence to others (whence Nielsen's »scharling-apologie« pamphlet). And it was Martensen who triggered the last act of Kierkegaard's authorship: his February 1854 memorial sermon placed Mynster as »et tjenende Led« in »den hellige Kjæde af Sandhedsvidner« — compressed by Kierkegaard into the »Sandhedsvidne« charge of his Fædrelandet attack (December 1854) and the »Øieblikket« campaign of 1855. Only the four controversy documents are catalogued below; the larger corpus — »Mester Eckart« (1840), »Grundrids til Moralphilosophiens System« (1841), »Den christelige Daab« (1843), »Den christelige Ethik« (1871–78), the memoirs »Af mit Levnet« (1882–83) — is left for later.

Den menneskelige Selvbevidstheds Autonomie i vor Tids dogmatiske Theologie (De autonomia conscientiae sui humanae)
1837; Danish transl. 1841. Kjøbenhavn; Latin original 1837, Danish transl. L. V. Petersen 1841
The licentiate dissertation, and the programmatic document of Danish speculative theology: against the autonomy of human self-consciousness in modern dogmatics — the principle Martensen finds running from Descartes through rationalism to Kant and Schleiermacher — and for a theonomous dogmatics in which self-consciousness knows itself only in dependence on God. The young Kierkegaard read it closely, and its picture of modern philosophy as beginning in doubt is part of what »Johannes Climacus eller De omnibus dubitandum est« mocks. Rasmus Nielsen (1809–1884)

Professor of philosophy at Copenhagen 1841–1884. Developed the central position of the mid-century debate: that faith and knowledge spring from absolutely heterogeneous principles. Target of both Brøchner's philosophical critique and Brandes's cultural polemic.

Den speculative Methodes Anvendelse paa den hellige Historie
1842. H.C. Klein, Copenhagen
Danish version, made by B. C. Bøggild, of Nielsen's 1840 Latin dissertation »De speculativa historiae sacrae tractandae methodo«. Body printed pp. 1–162, Fraktur. The earliest statement of the speculative programme he would spend the 1850s and 1860s dismantling from within — and, read against the later books, the clearest measure of how far he moved. Two Deele: the first on the relation of philosophy to history in general (§§ 1–10), the second on sacred history itself (§§ 11–23), running from Catholicism through Luther and the symbolic books to criticism, subjective and objective rationalism, and finally Strauß's panlogism, with a Slutning at p. 161. The §§ run continuously across both Parts. Bøggild's translator's preface sets the book against H. L. Martensen's »Den menneskelige Selvbevidstheds Autonomie« and refers the reader to the review in »Fædrelandet« Nr. 329 (1840); it is dated Kjøbenhavn, 24 January 1842. V. Klein & P. A. Rosenberg. 1909. København: Det Schønbergske Forlag (Ernst Bierberg & Michael H. Jensen), 1909; contents run to p. 111. Frontispiece portrait after H. W. Bissen.
Memorial volume for the centenary of Nielsen's birth (4 July 1809), dedicated »Til unge danske Mænd, som higer og søger« and prefaced with a Hostrup verse. Three parts. Hans Brøchner (1820–1875)

Professor of philosophy at Copenhagen 1857–1875. Post-Hegelian, moved toward a naturalistic humanism under the influence of Feuerbach and Strauss. With Rasmus Nielsen he is one of the two men who carried Kierkegaard into the Danish university in the decade after 1855: the authorship reached the next generation largely as these two professors taught and disputed it — Nielsen annexing it to his dualism of faith and knowledge, Brøchner reading it as a psychological and ethical achievement whose theology he rejected. He was also its first serious expositor. »Om Søren Kierkegaards Virksomhed som religieus Forfatter« appeared in Fædrelandet three weeks after the death, and the »Erindringer« of 1871–72 remain a primary source for the man. The exchange with Nielsen in 1868 (»Problemet om Tro og Viden« and the »Svar«) is accordingly a quarrel between two custodians of the same inheritance, not an attack from outside it. What he transmitted he transmitted mainly by teaching: Brandes and Høffding both sat in his lecture room, and Høffding credited Brøchner's lectures as the direct cause of his own conversion away from Nielsen's position.

Kristian Kroman (1846–1925)

Professor of philosophy at Copenhagen 1884–1922, in the chair he took over from Sophus Heegaard, and one of the three professors who between them ran the filosofikum for a generation — Høffding and Claudius Wilkens were the other two. He came to philosophy sideways: a schoolteacher's training at Jonstrup in 1865, then artium read on his own in 1869, and a turn to philosophy that Dansk Biografisk Leksikon credits to Kierkegaard's writings and to personal acquaintance with Hans Brøchner. His specialism was what we would call philosophy of science. »Vor Naturerkjendelse« (1883), which took the Academy's gold medal and was translated into German the same year, divides the sciences into formal and real, and grounds the certainty of the formal ones in a claim about what we can picture: »Al Tankenødvendighed beror til syvende og sidst paa Anskuelsesnødvendighed«. That premise is what put him at odds with the mathematics of his century — DBL names it as the reason for his polemical stance toward both the non-Euclidean geometries and Einstein's relativity theory. He is in this collection for the second of those quarrels. His 1915 attack on special relativity in Fysisk Tidsskrift set off the only sustained critical discussion the theory received in Denmark, and Høffding answered him by name in »Relation som Kategori« (1921), which we carry in both Danish and English. He also taught Bohr filosofikum. Kroman is otherwise best remembered outside philosophy altogether, as a school reformer and as the author of the gymnastics handbook that put a modified Swedish system into Danish schools.

Niels Bohr (1885–1962)

Niels Henrik David Bohr — physicist, and the figure toward whom this whole collection points. His 1913 quantum model of the atom and the principle of complementarity (Como, 1927) reshaped physics; the 1922 Nobel Prize recognized the former. But Bohr was also a philosopher of knowledge in the Danish tradition gathered here: raised in the circle around Harald Høffding — his father's friend and his own philosophical mentor — and a lifelong reader of Poul Martin Møller's »En dansk Students Eventyr«, whose licentiate, dizzy with the self that thinks about itself, Bohr loved to quote. From quantum theory he drew what he called an "epistemological lesson": that the conditions of observation enter irreducibly into what is observed, so that subject and object cannot be cleanly separated — the theme this catalog traces back through Høffding, Sibbern, Treschow and beyond. The collection's capstone is an edition of his philosophical writings in eight volumes — the four Danish »Filosofiske Skrifter« and the four English »Philosophical Writings« — listed separately below, since for the supplementary volume the Danish and English are independent selections rather than translations of one another. Its distinguishing feature is dual marginal pagination: each essay carries the page numbers of its original printing in a journal, congress volume or Festschrift alongside those of the collected volume scholars usually cite, so a passage can be located either way. Every piece links to its first printing, by DOI where one exists.

Filosoffen Niels Bohr
David Favrholdt. 2009. Informations Forlag, Copenhagen
Favrholdt's synthesis of his lifelong study of Bohr as thinker, arguing that Bohr was a genuine philosopher whose insight into quantum mechanics yielded a modern understanding of the limits of thought imposed by our language and concepts. David Favrholdt (1931–2012), Dr.phil. and professor at the University of Southern Denmark, heard Bohr lecture as a student and became the leading Danish interpreter of complementarity. A clean modern e-book (epub) edition exists, so this is a translation-only project — no transcription needed.
