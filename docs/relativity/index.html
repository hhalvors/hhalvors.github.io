No-go theorems for extended bodies

Born (1909) proposed a definition of the rigid body suited to the theory of relativity. Ehrenfest (1909) showed that a body rigid in this sense cannot be set into uniform rotation; Herglotz and Noether (1910) showed that the definition leaves a body only three degrees of freedom; and Laue (1911) argued that, since no action propagates faster than light, no body can have a finite number of degrees of freedom.

Gleichförmige Rotation starrer Körper und Relativitätstheorie
Uniform rotation of rigid bodies and the theory of relativity
Paul Ehrenfest (1880–1933). Physikalische Zeitschrift 10 (1909): 918
A one-page note arguing that relativistic rigidity cannot be maintained for a cylinder set into uniform rotation: its periphery should contract while its radius should not. The origin of what became known as the Ehrenfest paradox.

On the definition of the centre of gravity of an arbitrary closed system in the theory of relativity
Christian Møller (1904–1980). Communications of the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies, Series A, No. 5 (1949)
Shows that the centre of mass of a spinning body depends on the frame of reference: the centres for all frames fill a disc of radius S/Mc about the centre in the rest frame. A body with positive energy density cannot be smaller than this disc.
