title: "Werner Heisenberg"
author: Hans Halvorson and Anja Skaar Jacobsen
---

- Werner Heisenberg was Bohr's closest collaborator during the crucial
  time of consolidation of quantum mechanics (circa 1926).
  
- Heisenberg is (in)famous for proposing to eliminate all but
  measurable quantities in quantum mechanics. The resulting "theory"
  is called matrix mechanics.
  
- Heisenberg and Bohr had severe disagreements about the derivation
  and meaning of the uncertainty relations. See [@camilleri2007bohr]  
  
- Schrödinger and Heisenberg were at each other's necks in 1926. Bohr
  suggested that both of them had insights that needed to be taken
  account of in a final formulation of quantum mechanics.

- Heisenberg wrote detailed accounts of his interactions with Bohr,
  including philosophical discussions (see
  [@heisenberg2013teil]. However, the historical accuracy of some of
  Heisenberg's accounts (e.g. of their discussions) is somewhat
  questionable. Heisenberg seems frequently to interpret Bohr's
  philosophical statements through the lense of his own evolving
  philosophy of nature.
  
- Heisenberg was involved in Nazi Germany's atomic bomb project. Did
  he try to sabotage it? That's what he claimed after the war.
