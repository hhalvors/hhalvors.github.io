This page collects resources on operator algebras and their use in
quantum theory: the mathematics of C\*-algebras and von Neumann
algebras, the algebraic formulation of quantum mechanics, quantum
statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, and the use of von
Neumann algebras in quantum gravity since about 2021. It continues the
[mathematical side](/quantum.html#the-mathematical-side) of the guide
to the foundations of quantum mechanics.

Each entry is tagged. *Survey* means article length, read in a
sitting or two. *Textbook* means written to be learned from in order.
*Reference* means a treatise to be consulted. *Free* means legally
available online.

## Where to start

**Jonathan Sorce, "Notes on the type classification of von Neumann
algebras,"** *Reviews in Mathematical Physics* 36 (2024): 2430002
([doi](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129055X24300024)) --- *survey, free*
([arXiv:2302.01958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01958)). Why factors
are the basic case; the types I, II and III explained by whether the
algebra has pure states, a trace, or neither, and by what it takes to
renormalize an unnormalizable trace; standard forms; and the
connection with modular theory, including the argument that local
algebras in quantum field theory are type III~1~. Written for
physicists reading the recent quantum-gravity papers listed below; it
aims at intuition rather than precise definitions.

**Edward Witten, "Notes on some entanglement properties of quantum
field theory,"** *Reviews of Modern Physics* 90 (2018): 045003
([doi](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.90.045003)) --- *survey,
free* ([arXiv:1803.04993](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04993)). The
Reeh--Schlieder theorem, the modular operator, relative entropy and
its monotonicity, all motivated by the finite-dimensional case. Its
stated aim is to explain entanglement when it is a property of the
algebra of observables and not just of the state.

**Christopher Fewster and Kasia Rejzner, "Algebraic quantum field
theory --- an introduction,"** in Finster, Giulini, Kleiner and
Tolksdorf (eds.), *Progress and Visions in Quantum Theory in View of
Gravity* (Birkhäuser, 2020) --- *survey, free*
([arXiv:1904.04051](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04051)). The GNS
construction, unitarily inequivalent representations (worked through
the van Hove model), the axioms, the spectrum condition,
Reeh--Schlieder, the split property, the universal type of local
algebras and superselection sectors, with exercises.

**Detlev Buchholz and Klaus Fredenhagen, "Algebraic quantum field
theory: objectives, methods, and results"** --- *survey, free*
([arXiv:2305.12923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12923)). Written for
the *Encyclopedia of Mathematical Physics*. An
overview of the whole program by two of the people who built it.

**Hans Halvorson and Michael Müger, "Algebraic quantum field
theory,"** in Butterfield and Earman (eds.), *Philosophy of Physics*
(North-Holland, 2007): 731--864
([doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-044451560-5/50011-7)) ---
*survey, free* ([arXiv:math-ph/0602036](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0602036)).
Nonlocality, the particle and field concepts, and inequivalent
representations, followed by a detailed account of
Doplicher--Haag--Roberts superselection theory; Müger's appendix gives
the categorical background for the Doplicher--Roberts reconstruction
of the field algebra and gauge group.

**Vaughan Jones, *Von Neumann Algebras*** (lecture notes, Berkeley,
2009) --- *textbook, free*
([pdf](https://math.berkeley.edu/~vfr/VonNeumann2009.pdf)). The
fastest way into the mathematics. Built from semester courses; the
exercises carry part of the exposition, and the notes get to examples
and live topics with as little technical apparatus as possible.

## The mathematics

### Textbooks and treatises

**Richard Kadison and John Ringrose, *Fundamentals of the Theory of
Operator Algebras*** (4 vols., Academic Press, 1983--1992; vols. I--II
reprinted by the AMS, 1997) --- *textbook.* Volumes I and II are the
course; volumes III and IV work every exercise.

**Şerban Strătilă and László Zsidó, *Lectures on von Neumann
Algebras*** (2nd ed., Cambridge--IISc, 2019) --- *textbook.* The
first edition appeared in 1979 and went out of print quickly. The
second adds the factors of ICC groups, the Murray--von Neumann proof
that a type II~1~ factor has a trace, the first examples of type II~1~
and type III factors, and the theorems on algebras with a cyclic and
separating vector. Its sequel is **Şerban Strătilă, *Modular Theory in
Operator Algebras*** (2nd ed., Cambridge, 2020) --- *reference.*
First published in 1981, and until Takesaki's second volume the only
comprehensive monograph on the subject.

**Masamichi Takesaki, *Theory of Operator Algebras I--III*** (Springer,
Encyclopaedia of Mathematical Sciences 124, 125, 127; 2002--2003) ---
*reference.* Volume I reissues the 1979 book. Volume II is modular
theory, weights and crossed products; volume III, injective factors and
their classification.

**Bruce Blackadar, *Operator Algebras: Theory of C\*-Algebras and von
Neumann Algebras*** (Springer, Encyclopaedia of Mathematical Sciences
122, 2006) --- *reference.* One volume covering operators on Hilbert
space, C\*-algebras, von Neumann algebras, further structure, and
K-theory, with proofs often sketched rather than given. The quickest
place to look something up.

**Gert K. Pedersen, *C\*-Algebras and Their Automorphism Groups***
(2nd ed., ed. Søren Eilers and Dorte Olesen, Academic Press, 2018) ---
*reference.* The 1979 classic, which develops C\*-algebras as far as
possible without Hilbert space representations, extended with
commentary and a bibliography of the forty years since. The last two
chapters treat automorphism groups and their spectral theory: the
mathematics of dynamical systems on C\*-algebras.

**Jacques Dixmier, *C\*-Algebras*** (North-Holland, 1977) and ***Von
Neumann Algebras*** (North-Holland, 1981) --- *reference.* English
translations of the French originals of 1964 and 1957 (2nd ed. 1969).

**Karen Strung, *An Introduction to C\*-Algebras and the
Classification Program*** (Birkhäuser, 2021) --- *textbook.* Starts
from Banach algebras and ends at the Elliott invariant, the Jiang--Su
algebra, the Cuntz semigroup and the classification theorem for simple
nuclear C\*-algebras.

**Nathanial Brown and Narutaka Ozawa, *C\*-Algebras and
Finite-Dimensional Approximations*** (AMS, Graduate Studies in
Mathematics 88, 2008) --- *textbook.* Nuclearity, exactness, tensor
products of C\*-algebras and approximation properties: the background
for the Connes embedding problem below.

**Claire Anantharaman and Sorin Popa, *An Introduction to II~1~
Factors*** (book draft) --- *textbook, free*
([pdf](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~popa/Books/IIunV15.pdf)). Type II~1~
factors from the first examples onward, including Popa's
deformation/rigidity techniques.

### Modular theory and crossed products

**Fumio Hiai, *Lectures on Selected Topics in von Neumann Algebras***
(EMS Press, 2021) ([doi](https://doi.org/10.4171/ELM/32)) ---
*textbook, free* as an earlier version
([arXiv:2004.02383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02383)). A fast track
through the theory as Araki, Connes, Haagerup and Takesaki left it in
the 1970s: Tomita--Takesaki theory, the standard form, Connes cocycle
derivatives, operator-valued weights, Takesaki duality, Haagerup's
L^p^-spaces and spatial derivatives. The machinery behind relative
entropy in quantum field theory.

**Stephen J. Summers, "Tomita--Takesaki modular theory"** ---
*survey, free* ([arXiv:math-ph/0511034](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0511034)).
Written for the first edition of the *Encyclopedia of Mathematical
Physics* (Elsevier, 2006). The theorem, the KMS condition, and the
applications to physics, in a few pages.

**Hans-Jürgen Borchers, "On revolutionizing quantum field theory with
Tomita's modular theory,"** *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 41
(2000): 3604--3673 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.533323)) ---
*survey.* Half-sided modular inclusions, the Bisognano--Wichmann
theorem and the geometric action of modular groups, by one of its
main contributors.

**Dana P. Williams, *Crossed Products of C\*-Algebras*** (AMS,
Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 134, 2007) --- *textbook.* The
standard text on C\*-crossed products by locally compact groups:
covariant representations, imprimitivity theorems, induced
representations and ideal structure, Takai duality, and the reduced
crossed product. Assumes some familiarity with C\*-algebras and Morita
equivalence.

**Alfons van Daele, *Continuous Crossed Products and Type III von
Neumann Algebras*** (Cambridge, London Mathematical Society Lecture
Note Series 31, 1978) --- *textbook.* Sixty-eight pages. Part I is
crossed products of von Neumann algebras, the commutation theorem and
duality; part II shows that the crossed product of a type III algebra
by its modular automorphism group is semifinite, and derives the
structure of type III algebras from that. The construction Witten uses
in "Gravity and the crossed product," below.

### What has changed since the 1990s

1. **Classification of C\*-algebras.** The Elliott program for simple
   nuclear C\*-algebras reached its main classification theorem in the
   2010s. Strung's book above is the introduction.
2. **The Connes embedding problem was refuted, by way of Tsirelson's
   problem.** Zhengfeng Ji, Anand Natarajan, Thomas Vidick, John Wright
   and Henry Yuen, "MIP\* = RE," *Communications of the ACM* 64 (2021),
   no. 11; full version
   [arXiv:2001.04383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04383). The set of
   quantum correlations obtainable from tensor products of Hilbert
   spaces is strictly smaller, even after closure, than the set
   obtainable from commuting operators on a single Hilbert space. That
   is, the two standard ways of formalizing independent subsystems
   come apart --- a question about the Bell correlations treated in
   the [nonlocality section](/quantum.html#nonlocality) of the
   quantum-foundations guide.
3. **Rigidity for type II~1~ factors.** Popa's deformation/rigidity
   theory has produced classification results for the factors of
   groups and group actions. Anantharaman and Popa above is the
   introduction.
4. **Modular theory in high-energy physics.** Since Witten's 2018
   review, Tomita--Takesaki theory and the type classification have
   become working tools in quantum gravity. See the last section.

## Algebraic quantum theory

**Ola Bratteli and Derek W. Robinson, *Operator Algebras and Quantum
Statistical Mechanics*** (2 vols., 2nd ed., Springer, 1987 and 1997)
--- *reference.* Volume 1 is C\*- and von Neumann algebras, modular
theory and decomposition theory; volume 2 is KMS states, quantum spin
systems, and the CCR and CAR algebras.

**Rudolf Haag, *Local Quantum Physics*** (2nd ed., Springer, 1996) ---
*textbook.* The program set out by its founder.

**Huzihiro Araki, *Mathematical Theory of Quantum Fields*** (Oxford,
1999) --- *textbook.* Translated from the Japanese edition of 1993.

**Gérard Emch, *Algebraic Methods in Statistical Mechanics and Quantum
Field Theory*** (Wiley, 1972; Dover reprint, 2009) --- *textbook.*

**Klaas Landsman, *Foundations of Quantum Theory: From Classical
Concepts to Operator Algebras*** (Springer, 2017) --- *reference,
free* ([open access](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-51777-3)).
Appendix C is an introduction to operator algebras. See the
[entry in the quantum-foundations guide](/quantum.html#the-mathematical-side).

**Miklós Rédei and Stephen J. Summers, "Quantum probability
theory,"** *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 38
(2007): 390--417 --- *survey, free*
([arXiv:quant-ph/0601158](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0601158)).
Projection lattices of von Neumann algebras as non-commutative
probability spaces, and the Murray--von Neumann type classification
read as a classification of probability theories.

**Laura Ruetsche, *Interpreting Quantum Theories*** (Oxford, 2011) ---
*textbook.* Inequivalent representations in quantum field theory and
quantum statistical mechanics, and what they do to the question of
what a theory says.

**Stefan Hollands and Ko Sanders, *Entanglement Measures and Their
Properties in Quantum Field Theory*** (Springer, SpringerBriefs in
Mathematical Physics 34, 2018)
([doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-94902-4)) --- *textbook.*
Entanglement measures for local algebras, with upper and lower bounds,
and the mathematics introduced as needed.

**Kasia Rejzner, *Perturbative Algebraic Quantum Field Theory***
(Springer, Mathematical Physics Studies, 2016) --- *textbook.*
Perturbative quantum field theory done rigorously in the algebraic
framework, without divergent quantities.

**Romeo Brunetti, Claudio Dappiaggi, Klaus Fredenhagen and Jakob
Yngvason (eds.), *Advances in Algebraic Quantum Field Theory***
(Springer, Mathematical Physics Studies, 2015) --- *reference.*

### Measurement

**Christopher Fewster and Rainer Verch, "Quantum fields and local
measurements,"** *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 378 (2020):
851--889 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-020-03800-6)) ---
*free.* One quantum field, the probe, measures another, the system;
everything local and covariant, on curved spacetimes. The authors
present it as closing a long-open gap between quantum measurement
theory and algebraic quantum field theory.

**Christopher Fewster, "Lectures on measurement in quantum field
theory"** ([arXiv:2504.17437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17437)) and
**Rainer Verch, "Lecture notes on operator algebras and quantum field
theory"** ([arXiv:2507.00900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00900)) ---
*survey, free.* Companion courses from the 2025 EMS--IAMP spring
school. Verch covers modular theory, the Bisognano--Wichmann and
Borchers theorems, locally covariant quantum field theory, and
temperature and entropy near black holes.

## Quantum gravity and the type of the algebra

**Edward Witten, "Gravity and the crossed product,"** *Journal of High
Energy Physics* 2022, no. 10: 008
([open access](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP10(2022)008)). Leutheusser
and Liu found an emergent type III~1~ algebra in large-N gauge theory;
Witten shows that 1/N corrections make it type II~∞~, the crossed
product of the type III~1~ algebra by its modular automorphism group,
and that black-hole entropy is then defined up to an additive
constant.

**Venkatesa Chandrasekaran, Roberto Longo, Geoff Penington and Edward
Witten, "An algebra of observables for de Sitter space,"** *Journal of
High Energy Physics* 2023, no. 2: 082
([open access](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP02(2023)082)). Operators in
a static patch, dressed to an observer's worldline, form a type II~1~
algebra; empty de Sitter space is its state of maximum entropy.

**Edward Witten, "A background-independent algebra in quantum
gravity,"** *Journal of High Energy Physics* 2024, no. 3: 077
([open access](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP03(2024)077)). The
algebra of operators along an observer's worldline, proposed as an
algebra that does not presuppose a background spacetime.

**Hong Liu, "Lectures on entanglement, von Neumann algebras, and
emergence of spacetime"** --- *survey, free*
([arXiv:2510.07017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07017)). Expanded from
lectures at TASI 2023 and elsewhere. The first half is von Neumann
algebras and their classification as a theory of entanglement; the
second, the algebraic formulation of AdS/CFT at large N,
subregion--subalgebra duality, and gravitational entropy.
