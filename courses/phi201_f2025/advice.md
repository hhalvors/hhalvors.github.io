Hello, PHI 201 students. My name is Dave Achonu, and I took PHI 201 in the fall of the 2025-26 academic year. I took this class because I am a prospective philosophy minor, and I felt that developing the skills associated with classical logic would be helpful in my future philosophical endeavors and would make me a better thinker overall. 

After completing the course, I have 3 major pieces of advice. Of course, all of the typical "go to lecture" and "go to precept" advice applies, but these 3 are what helped me the most. 

Read the textbook. I know that for some courses, the textbook is not super helpful, or simply skimming the textbook is sufficient. But for this course, especially later, when the content gets challenging, even after lecture and precept, some of the concepts will be hard to grasp.  Sitting down and really reading the textbook and following along with the worked examples is helpful.

Do practice problems. Do all the practice problems you can, especially for proofs. Do all the ones in the textbook and in any practice exams. If you run out, ask Dr. Halvorson for more, and he will happily provide.  

Ask everyone you can for help. I went to the office hours with my preceptor weekly, I would bug my friends for help, and I would often email my preceptor and Dr. Halvorson. When doing practice problems, especially if you're doing proofs, you may get stuck, and the best thing to do is ask for help. See how different people solve different problems and take their strategies to develop heuristics that help you approach future problems.
